The interactive inventory covers 35 MS system configurations across 9 instrument families. You can toggle between the system inventory table and the component frequency distributions, and filter by family. Click any row for the full 5-component breakdown including ion optics.

The key patterns that emerge from the frequency distributions:

Sample Introduction — solution nebulization and GC separation are the most common; laser ablation and ion sputtering follow. The diversity here is highest of any component, confirming that sample introduction is the most modular part of the system.

Ion Source — ICP dominates (7 configurations), followed by electron ionization (spanning GC-MS and IRMS families) and ion sputtering (SIMS family). Resonance ionization and field ionization are niche.

Mass Analyzer — magnetic sector is the most frequent across all Earth science MS, appearing in TIMS, MC-ICP-MS, SIMS, IRMS, noble gas, and AMS. Quadrupole is second (ICP-MS and GC-MS). TOF is growing. FT-ICR and Orbitrap are rare in geoscience.

Detector — electron multiplier is the most common single detector type; Faraday + EM combinations dominate isotope ratio work; multi-collector Faraday arrays are specific to high-precision isotope ratio instruments.

A few things I flagged as uncertain and that could be refined with deeper research: the relative prevalence ratings are my qualitative estimates, not from a systematic census. The actual global inventory of, say, NanoSIMS instruments (~30–40 worldwide) versus quadrupole ICP-MS instruments (thousands) represents orders of magnitude difference that the "Medium" vs. "Very High" labels only roughly capture. A deeper version of this project could pull installation counts from manufacturer reports or facility databases.
